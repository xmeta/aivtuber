= EXP-0006: Irodori-TTS v4.1-Small SUN and LUNA as an optional local TTS backend (#409)

:toc:
:toclevels: 2

This is a durable experiment record in the link:README.adoc[Durable Experiment Records] format.
It is *step 1* of #409, which at the time of writing carries `status:design-ready`: an evaluation, with throwaway
scripts only.
No production code, no new dependency in the repository, and no model, binary or audio is committed.
The default voice stays Supertonic 3 int8 on sherpa-onnx (link:EXP-0003-japanese-tts-bench.adoc[EXP-0003]); this
record asks whether the two Irodori-TTS checkpoints are a viable *optional* second backend, in the shape #295 gave
VOICEVOX (a separate loopback process the operator installs, nothing bundled).

== 2. Result summary

All numbers were measured here unless marked as read from a source.

* *The usable configuration is LUNA with both the DiT and the codec in bf16 on the GPU*: p50 1.13 s, p95 1.36 s per reply (mean audio 4.72 s), RTF 0.245, peak 3.1 GB of VRAM, load 94 s. Deterministic: 50 of 50 replies identical in a second pass in the same process and 10 of 10 in a fresh process; no error in 50 replies or 38 handling items; 48 kHz.
* *SUN is not a live-reply candidate*: p50 2.85 s, p95 3.33 s (RTF 0.66) in fp32, and *slower* in its own bf16 recipe (p50 3.46 s, RTF 0.75), because the codec stays fp32 and the casts cost more than the smaller DiT saves. Also deterministic (50/50).
* *LUNA fp32 has a heavy tail*: p50 1.19 s but p95 6.86 s (one reply took 25.5 s), RTF 0.585. The p50 is close to bf16; the tail is not. Both LUNA rows use 4 NFE, so the tail is not a sampler effect, but its cause was not isolated (section 8).
* *Step count is nearly free; the codec is the bottleneck.* 4 NFE against 24 steps on GPU fp32 changed the mean by 11% (2.76 s against 3.10 s), while switching the codec (and DiT) to bf16 on LUNA cut it by 58% (1.16 s against 2.76 s). A faster sampler alone cannot make SUN live-capable.
* *CPU is out on this host.* SUN with the card's recipe took *about 150 s* for one 19-character reply (3.36 s of audio, RTF ~45) when run through the checkpoint's own synthesize.py; a 3-reply harness run did not finish its second pass within 25 minutes. LUNA, which needs only 4 NFE, produced *no reply at all within about 15 minutes* of the same harness. This CPU (Zen 2) has no hardware bf16, and the cards' "RTF 1.110 on 8 CPU cores" (SUN) and "0.602" (LUNA) were not reproduced; those numbers were obtained on an unidentified VM and are not evidence for this project's hosts.
* *Load and footprint.* Load (checkpoint + codec + tokenizer, cold process) 57.5 s (SUN fp32), 59.9 s (LUNA fp32), 67.0 s (SUN bf16), 93.5 s (LUNA bf16); first call 16.8 / 7.1 / 4.2 / 1.4 s; peak VRAM 3.9 / 3.8 / 2.9 / 3.1 GB; peak process RSS 6.4-6.9 GB. The recommended LUNA bf16 row (3.1 GB) plus the default Thinking model's about 2.6 GB is roughly 5.7 GB of an 8 GB card; SUN fp32 is about 6.5 GB. Those are sums of separately measured figures, not a co-residency measurement (Q3).
* *Sample rate is 48 kHz* (the DACVAE codec), against 44.1 kHz for the shipped Supertonic path.
* *Intelligibility is the negative result.* The same round-trip instrument that gave the shipped engine 4.0% reading CER gives every Irodori configuration about 12.4-13.5% (text CER 15.8-18.1% against 8.2%), with 11-15 of 50 replies above 30% text CER. The scored handling set is nearly clean (3-5 of 32), and the reply errors are *lost first words* rather than misread kanji (section 6.2): the audio starts within 18-32 ms of the first sample, and `こんにちは、…` came back as `…今日も来てくれてありがとう`, `わあ` as `うわ`, `あはは` as `ハハ`. The instrument cannot say whether the model clips the onset or the STT is weak on this voice, but neither explanation supports calling this voice better than the shipped one.
* *Licences are MIT end to end* (code, both checkpoints, base model, MF model, Yomi vector, codec weights, text encoder) with Apache-2.0 for the DACVAE code and MIT for SilentCipher; the base model card carries *ethical restrictions* (no impersonation, no deepfakes, no misinformation, a "may resemble a real person by chance" disclaimer) that the project must state (section 3.2).

== 4. Workload and method

=== 4.1 Texts

The 50 replies and the 38-item handling set of EXP-0003, reused unchanged from `examples/benchmarks/tts/corpus.py`
(12-43 characters per reply, mean 26; 32 scored handling items plus 2 URL, 1 symbol and 3 emoji items).
They were written for one VTuber persona and one annotator's expected readings; nothing was added for this bench.

=== 4.2 What is timed

* *Call time:* wall time of one `InferenceRuntime.synthesize()` call (Python `perf_counter`), including text
  normalization, tokenization, duration prediction, sampling, codec decode and WAV write. No engine streams, so
  the time to first audio equals the call time: about 1.1 s (LUNA bf16) to 3.5 s (SUN bf16) after the text is final.
  *Per-phrase latency is this call time:* every corpus item is one phrase, which is one request in the project's
  phrase mode, so a reply of several phrases costs one call each.
* *RTF* is the sum of call times over the sum of audio durations (below 1 means faster than real time).
* *Load time:* `InferenceRuntime.from_key` plus `speed_patch.apply` (checkpoint load, codec download/load,
  tokenizer fetch, weight folding/fusing). *First call* is the first synthesis after load; three more warm-ups
  are not recorded.
* *Peak memory:* resident set of the harness process sampled every 25 ms, and peak CUDA allocation
  (`torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated`) sampled at the same rate; both are allocator figures, not driver totals.
* *Determinism:* SHA-256 of the audio as 16-bit PCM for the same text, (a) a second pass in the same process,
  (b) a fresh process (run1 against run2, first 10 replies).

=== 4.3 Round-trip intelligibility proxy

Unchanged from EXP-0003: each wav is resampled to 16 kHz, padded with 0.3 s of silence before and 0.5 s after,
transcribed by sherpa-onnx ReazonSpeech k2-v2 int8 (greedy, 4 threads); two micro-averaged CERs over the replies
with a 95% bootstrap interval (2000 resamples, seed 20261110): *text* CER (orthographic, after the EXP-0002
normalisation) and *reading* CER (both sides converted to katakana by Open JTalk). Handling items are scored by
reading CER against the hand-written expected reading; above 0.25 counts as a misreading.
*This measures what the STT recovers from the audio, not naturalness, prosody or voice quality.*

=== 5.2 Reading the table

* *One configuration is usable for live replies.* LUNA with its own bf16 recipe answers in about 1.1 s for a
  4.7 s reply; the p95 of 1.36 s means a producer of 20-character replies hears the voice about 1.1-1.4 s
  after the text is final. That is slower than the shipped Supertonic path (0.9 s p50 CPU) but in the same
  order; whether the larger model sounds better is exactly what this record cannot answer (section 6).
* *SUN is a pre-rendering option at best.* 2.85 s (fp32) or 3.46 s (bf16) per reply is 2.5-3 times the shipped
  engine's call time and roughly doubles end-to-end latency once the Thinking and gate stages are added.
* *bf16 is not a free switch.* On SUN it made things *worse* (3.46 s against 2.85 s): the codec stays fp32 and
  the fused attention converts back to fp32, so the smaller datatype pays conversion without shrinking the part
  that dominates. On LUNA it is applied to both halves and cuts time by 58%.
* *fp32 LUNA's p50 hides a tail.* One reply took 25.5 s and another 14.5 s while the median stayed at 1.19 s.
  Nothing in the request differs (20 and 14 characters); the harness was alone on the GPU apart from a log read.
  The cause was not isolated (allocator growth or a codec kernel falling back to a slow path are candidates);
  it is the reason the recommendation names bf16 rather than "either precision".
* *The sampler is cheap; the codec is not.* LUNA's 4 NFE against SUN's 24 steps changes the GPU fp32 mean by
  11%, while the bf16 codec cut LUNA's mean by 58%. Any future speed work should target the DACVAE decoder or
  a streaming/chunked decode, not the flow steps.

=== 5.3 Load, footprint and devices

Cold load is 57-94 s, dominated by reading a 3 GB safetensors checkpoint and building/folding the modules;
the codec (410 MB) is downloaded on first use and cached afterwards. VRAM peaks at 2.9-3.9 GB with 48 kHz
output, and the process adds 6.4-6.9 GB of resident memory.

*Next to the default Thinking model:* `docs/local-profile-quickstart.adoc` gives the default Thinking model
(Gemma 4 E4B QAT) about 2.6 GB of VRAM, so the recommended LUNA bf16 configuration (3.1 GB) sums to about
5.7 GB of an 8 GB card and SUN fp32 (3.9 GB) to about 6.5 GB. That is an addition of two separately measured
allocator figures, not a measurement of both loaded together; the optional `qwen3-4b` (2.5 GB file) is closer.
What does not fit on an 8 GB card is the perception profile, which the same document puts at about 12 GB VRAM.
The operator-facing consequence is Q3.

=== 5.4 Determinism

All four GPU configurations are bit-identical for the same text: 50 of 50 in a second pass in the same process,
10 of 10 in a fresh process. The seed and the pinned checkpoints are enough; nothing needed a sampling repeat.
No path in this record is unseeded.

=== 6.2 Why the CER is high: lost onsets, not misread kanji

The handling set is nearly clean (3-5 of 32, comparable to the shipped engine's 6 and VOICEVOX's 2), and the
items that fail are the ones EXP-0003 already flagged as instrument problems: `1日目` came back as `一日目`
(the STT cannot separate ついたち from いちにち), and `Thank you、また来てね。` came back as `また来てね`
(the English phrase is missing from the audio or from the transcription). One kanji item (`辛い物が好きです。`)
came back as a single syllable. The scored handling set therefore does *not* show a kanji-reading problem.

The reply CER is a different picture. Reading the transcripts, several replies lose their *first word* rather
than mispronouncing later text: `こんにちは、今日も来てくれてありがとう！` came back as
`今日も来てくれてありがとう`, `わあ、すごい！` as `うわ、すごい`, `あはは、それは面白いね。` as
`ハハ、それは面白いね`. The generated audio starts speaking within *18-32 ms* of the first sample, with no
leading silence at all, which is the one measurable correlate: either the model truncates the onset or the STT
loses it. This instrument cannot separate the two, and EXP-0003's STT warning applies with full force here: a
0.3 s silence pad before the utterance did not recover the words. *A listening check is required* before the
gap is attributed to the model.

=== 6.3 Non-speech text (raw engine behaviour)

* URL, symbol and emoji items are handled the way every engine in EXP-0003 handled them: URLs are *spoken* as letter noise, emoji and kaomoji are silently dropped by the model's normalizer. ADR-0004 D-5 (drop them before TTS) stays necessary and stays upstream, so a production path never sends them.
* Nothing here justifies changing ADR-0004's projection or adding a kanji-reading front end for Irodori.

=== 6.4 Voice identity and cloning (not measured)

The record ran `no_ref` only. The checkpoint supports zero-shot cloning from a 30 s+ reference clip and a
caption path for voice design; neither was measured, because #409 leaves reference-clip cloning as an open
decision (section 10, Q1). Note the licence asymmetry: an unconditioned model gives a *different speaker per
seed*, so a persona would need a fixed seed or an operator-supplied reference clip to sound stable across runs.

*Offline behaviour:* first load downloads the codec (410 MB) and tokenizer (8 MB) from the Hub. The checkpoint
metadata carries the tokenizer repository, so an air-gapped install must pre-seed the HF cache with both; a
deployment note is required. Nothing downloads at *build* time, and the project's own pipeline is unaffected.

== 8. Limitations

. *No naturalness evidence.* No listening test, MOS, prosody, accent or voice-quality judgement, and no human listener. The round-trip CER shows an STT can recover the words.
. *The voice is not fixed in this record.* Every row ran `no_ref` with one seed, so each configuration is one sampled speaker, and a different seed is a different voice. A persona decision cannot be made from this record.
. *STT errors count against the TTS,* and the ReazonSpeech instrument has its own error floor on synthetic speech (EXP-0002), so absolute CER is pessimistic and the ordering among the four configurations is within noise.
. *The 13%-against-4.0% reading gap is not attributable.* The transcripts show lost first words, and the audio has no leading silence (onset 18-32 ms), which is consistent with both a model artefact and an STT weakness on this voice. It is too large to ignore and too ambiguous to blame on the model; only a listener can settle it.
. *CPU rows are incomplete and confounded.* SUN was measured with one utterance through the checkpoint's own script; the 3-reply harness run did not reach its second pass in 25 minutes, and LUNA produced no reply in about 15 minutes, both at 4 threads. bf16 on a Zen 2 CPU is emulated, which is the likely dominant cause, and *fp32 CPU was not measured* (one attempt was abandoned for time). "CPU is out" therefore means "on a CPU without hardware bf16, with the cards' bf16 recipes".
. *One GPU, one run per configuration* (run2 repeats only the first 10 replies), on a machine that also ran monitoring commands. The LUNA fp32 tail (25.5 s, 14.5 s) was not reproduced or explained.
. *The cards' benchmark method is not reproduced.* Their CER uses kotoba-whisper and a different sentence set, their RTF a different CPU; the comparison in section 3.3 is informative, not a replication.
. *Watermarking was off* (silentcipher not installed), so the record says nothing about the watermark's latency or its effect on the round trip. The code paths exist.
. *Not measured:* voice cloning, captions/voice design, streaming or chunked synthesis, quantized variants, concurrency (two syntheses at once, or a synthesis next to Thinking), long replies beyond 43 characters, and the official server itself (only its README was read).
. *Licences were read from cards, LICENSE files and repository metadata, not from legal advice.* Training data of the checkpoints, the codec and ModernBERT-JA was not read.

== 9. Protocol deviations

* *SUN CPU is a single utterance* through the checkpoint's own `synthesize.py` with `--threads 4`, timed end to
  end (~250 s including ~100 s of load, 3.36 s of audio); the harness rows for CPU could not be completed.
* *LUNA CPU produced no output* in about 15 minutes and was stopped; no number is reported for it rather than a
  zero.
* *Handling set on CPU:* not run (the reply rows alone did not finish).
* *The second in-process pass* was run for every GPU row but not for the CPU rows.
* *run2* repeats the first 10 replies and, because the runner passes `--handling-limit 0`, also the 38 handling
  items; only the replies are compared.
* *torch 2.11.0* instead of the `<2.11` the official pyproject pins; `sentencepiece` was not installed (the
  tokenizer does not need it on this checkpoint); `silentcipher` was not installed.
* *No model card reproduction:* the cards' own benchmark scripts were not run beyond `synthesize.py`.

== 10. Open questions

. *Q1 (voice identity).* Irodori has no named voice. Does the operator fix a seed, supply their own reference
   clip, or use captions? A reference clip brings consent, provenance and disclosure duties with it (the same
   question #409 leaves open).
. *Q2 (default remains Supertonic).* Is a GPU-only, 1.1 s-per-reply optional backend worth a second install
   path next to VOICEVOX, or should Irodori wait until the voice-quality comparison (Q4) shows it is better?
   Listing it in the panel's voice section (#409's second open decision) follows only after the backend works
   from configuration, as VOICEVOX does; this record does not propose a panel change.
. *Q3 (VRAM budget).* The backend needs 2.9-3.9 GB of VRAM and 6.4-6.9 GB of RSS. Against the default Thinking
   model's ~2.6 GB that sums to ~5.7 GB (LUNA bf16) of an 8 GB card, which looks feasible but was not measured
   with both loaded; the ~12 GB perception profile does not fit. Does the product support Irodori on an 8 GB
   card next to `default`, only on a larger card, or not at all?
. *Q4 (listening test).* Nobody has listened to SUN or LUNA, and the round-trip reading CER is about 13%
   against the shipped engine's 4.0% (section 6.1). A small blinded comparison against Supertonic and VOICEVOX,
   plus a check of whether the first word is actually audible, is the gate for this backend; without it the
   issue should stay design-only.
. *Q5 (bridge).* Wrap the official server behind a `voicevox_bridge`-style translator, or add an
   OpenAI-`/v1/audio/speech` code path to the existing HTTP adapter? The first touches one place; the second
   broadens a provider-neutral adapter with a provider-shaped API.
. *Q6 (CPU later).* If a bf16-capable or AVX-512 CPU appears, or a quantized variant lands, the CPU question
   should be re-measured rather than assumed from this record.
. *Q7 (watermark and disclosure).* SilentCipher is MIT and the card's code watermarks when it is installed.
   Does the operator-facing notice (and the stream) state that the voice is synthetic, and is the watermark a
   requirement of the backend?
