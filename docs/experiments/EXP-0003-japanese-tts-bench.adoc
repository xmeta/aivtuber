= EXP-0003: Japanese text-to-speech engine bench (#220 S2a)

:toc:
:toclevels: 2

This is a durable experiment record in the link:README.adoc[Durable Experiment Records] format.
It is the evaluation slice S2a of #220: it chooses a local Japanese text-to-speech (TTS) engine by measurement before any implementation (S2b).
It contains measurement and throwaway scripts only: no production code, no new dependency in the repository, no model, binary or audio committed.
It follows link:EXP-0002-japanese-stt-bench.adoc[EXP-0002] (the STT engine reused here for a round-trip proxy) and link:../adr/0004-speech-lifecycle-and-text-projection.adoc[ADR-0004] (display-to-speech text projection, whose assumption A-SPEECH-002 this bench partly tests).

Decision question:
  Which local, permissively licensed Japanese TTS engine, and which integration path from Rust without
  build-time network downloads, should #220 S2b implement behind the existing normalized HTTP TTS contract
  so that the +local+ profile (loopback endpoints only) has a zero-paid-dependency voice?

Context / baseline:
  The generative pipeline requires a TTS endpoint (+AIVTUBER_TTS_ENDPOINT+, normalized contract: request
  {text, style, voice_model, viseme_mapping}, response {audio_ref, duration_ms, viseme_ref?}; audio is returned by
  reference, +audio://+ below +AIVTUBER_AUDIO_ROOT+). S1 of #220 (merged) used a stub TTS. docs/reuse-first.adoc

== 2. Result summary

All numbers are measured here unless marked as read from a source.
Latency is the wall time of one synthesis call on CPU (4 threads) over 50 replies, after one first call and three warm-ups.
No engine delivered audio before the end of the call, so time to first audio equals the call time (section 6.2).
The round trip is an intelligibility proxy, not naturalness (section 10).

* *Fastest and smallest: sherpa-onnx Supertonic 3 int8.* p50 0.89 s, p95 1.68 s per reply (mean audio 4.18 s, RTF 0.235), load 1.1 s, first call 0.78 s, peak RSS 364 MB (Python process included). Seeded runs were identical in 50 of 50 replies within a process and across two fresh processes.
* *Lowest reading error: VOICEVOX 0.26.1 (separate CPU engine, ずんだもん).* p50 1.35 s, p95 2.52 s (RTF 0.356), load 3.1 s, peak 714 MB; reading CER 2.3% (95% interval 1.1-4.0) against 4.0% (2.2-6.3) for Supertonic: the intervals overlap. It misread 2 of 32 scored handling items against 6 of 32 for Supertonic. Identical in 50 of 50 replies, in-process and across processes.
* *Kokoro-82M (kokoro-onnx + misaki ja phonemes, voice jf_alpha)* is usable only with the Open JTalk G2P stack: fp32 p50 1.35 s (RTF 0.43), 751 MB, reading CER 4.1% (2.1-6.8), but 11 of 32 handling misreadings (all 8 English-word items), *not repeatable* (0 of 50 identical, even in-process; lengths differ), and the int8 export took p50 13.6 s (RTF 4.3) on this CPU. The G2P wheels needed a workaround on Windows (section 11) and kokoro-onnx pulls GPL-3.0 espeak-ng components (section 3.2).
* *sherpa-onnx's own Kokoro export cannot read Japanese:* every reply came out as garbage (CER 97.8%), characters were skipped as out-of-vocabulary. Rejected.
* *Language must be passed explicitly to sherpa-onnx Supertonic:* the default language is +en+; leaving it unset raised text CER from 8.2% to 14.7% and reading CER from 4.0% to 7.9% (50 replies, intervals overlapping, direction consistent). *Seed must be passed too:* unseeded generation was not repeatable (0 of 50 identical in-process and across processes).
* *Text handling (32 scored items, section 6.4):* misreadings by the proxy: VOICEVOX 2, Supertonic 6, Kokoro 11. Supertonic's errors were English brand words (+YouTube+, +Twitter+/+X+, +VTuber+) and one kanji word (海老); the others read ordinary numbers and kanji. URLs were spoken as garbled letters by every engine and emoji were silently dropped: ADR-0004 D-5 (drop them before TTS) is therefore required, not optional.
* *Rust:* the +sherpa-onnx+ 2.0.1 crate drives Supertonic through +OfflineTts::generate_with_config+ (with an +extra+ map for +lang+ and +seed+). A scratch binary built offline in 56 s from the pinned library archive produced *float32 output bit-identical to the Python run in 50 of 50 replies*, load 0.93 s warm, p50 0.90 s. No new crate: the repository's existing +cargo deny --locked --features sherpa-stt check+ passes (advisories, bans, licenses, sources ok).
* *Licenses (section 3.2):* sherpa-onnx code Apache-2.0; Supertonic weights *OpenRAIL-M* (use-based restrictions including "publish AI-generated content without clearly disclosing it is machine-generated", with flow-down duties) and an MIT sample code license; VOICEVOX engine LGPL-3.0 (dual) with a mandatory *credit line* and *per-character terms* (some characters are non-commercial or need individual contact); Kokoro weights Apache-2.0 but four of five Japanese voices are CC BY and the card says some training audio was synthetic from closed TTS services; Style-Bert-VITS2 code *AGPL-3.0*; the Piper Japanese voice is *CC BY-NC-SA*.
* *Recommendation:* section 8.

Findings that need a decision from the project owner:

* *OpenRAIL-M for Supertonic 3* (new to the project): permissive for commercial use but with enforceable use restrictions that must be passed downstream, including a duty to disclose that the voice is machine-generated. Q1.
* *VOICEVOX:* mandatory credit line and per-character terms; the character must be chosen with the license in view and the persona's relation to that character (a VTuber with her own identity speaking with a third-party mascot's voice) is a product question. Q4.
* *GPL in the Kokoro path:* the Python tool chain around Kokoro (espeak-ng, phonemizer) is GPL; sherpa-onnx 2.0.1 is not affected. Another reason not to recommend the kokoro-onnx route.
* *Kokoro's data:* synthetic audio from closed TTS providers is part of its training set per its own card; the terms of those providers' outputs are not stated.
* *Attribution:* four of the five Kokoro Japanese voices are CC BY; VOICEVOX needs the credit line; the Open JTalk/HTS stack inside VOICEVOX carries a CC BY 3.0 voice ("Mei").

== 4. Workload and method

=== 4.1 Texts

* *Replies:* 50 short VTuber-style Japanese replies (greetings, reactions, thanks, small talk), 12-43 characters (mean 26), written for this bench. The planned range was 5-60 characters; the shortest and longest items are inside it but the bench does not cover the ends (section 11).
* *Text-handling set:* 38 items: 10 numbers, dates and counters, 8 English words and brands, 14 kanji-reading traps, 2 URLs, 1 symbol string, 3 emoji/kaomoji items. The 32 items of the first three groups have a hand-written expected reading in hiragana (one annotator, not independently reviewed). URL, symbol and emoji items have none: ADR-0004 D-5 drops them before TTS, so the bench only records what a raw engine does.

=== 4.2 What is timed

* *Call time:* wall time of one synthesis call (Python +perf_counter+). For VOICEVOX it is the two HTTP calls (+/audio_query+ then +/synthesis+) over loopback including WAV decoding in the harness. For kokoro-onnx it includes the Open JTalk G2P. *Time to first audio* is the call time: sherpa-onnx's callback fired once per generated chunk and Japanese text of at most 120 characters is a single chunk, so the first callback arrived at the end of the call (median 0.882 s against 0.893 s). Nothing streamed.
* *RTF* is the sum of call times over the sum of audio durations (below 1 means faster than real time).
* *Load time:* constructing the engine (for VOICEVOX: spawn until +/version+ answers). *Peak RSS* is the peak resident set of the harness process and its children sampled every 25 ms (Python, numpy and onnxruntime are part of the figure for in-process engines; for VOICEVOX it is the engine process tree). *First call* is the first synthesis after load; three more warm-ups are not recorded.
* *Determinism:* sha256 of the audio as 16-bit PCM for the same text (a) a second pass in the same process, (b) a fresh process (run1 against run2).

=== 4.3 Round-trip intelligibility proxy

Each wav is resampled to 16 kHz, padded with 0.3 s of silence before and 0.5 s after, and transcribed by sherpa-onnx ReazonSpeech k2-v2 int8 (the engine merged in #272; greedy, 4 threads). Two CERs over the 50 replies, micro-averaged, with a 95% bootstrap interval over replies (2000 resamples, seed 20261110):

. *Text CER:* hypothesis against the input text after the EXP-0002 normalisation (NFKC, lower-case, drop P/S/Z/C categories). It penalises harmless spelling choices (25 against 二十五, kanji against kana).
. *Reading CER:* both sides converted to katakana readings with Open JTalk (pyopenjtalk g2p), then compared. It ignores spelling but both sides carry Open JTalk's own reading errors, and the STT cannot separate readings that share a spelling (一日 as ついたち or いちにち, 二十歳 as はたち or にじゅっさい).

For the handling set, an item is a *misreading* when the reading CER of the hypothesis against the expected hiragana reading is above 0.25, or the call failed.
*This measures what the STT recovers from the audio. It is a proxy for intelligibility, not for naturalness, prosody, accent or voice quality, and STT errors count against the TTS.*

=== 5.2 Reading the table

* *Latency.* Supertonic is the fastest (RTF 0.24, 0.9 s per reply), VOICEVOX and Kokoro fp32 are about 1.5 times slower (RTF 0.36 and 0.43). All three are comfortably faster than real time for 4-second replies on 4 CPU threads, but none streams, so the time to first sound is the whole synthesis: about 0.9 s (Supertonic), 1.3 s (VOICEVOX) after the text is final. S1 measured 0.51-0.63 s from event to the TTS request, so event-to-sound is roughly 1.5-2 s for these engines before playback starts; this is a sum of separately measured figures, not an end-to-end run.
* *Cost.* Supertonic is the smallest (about 99 M parameters, 128 MB archive, 364 MB resident), VOICEVOX needs a 2.2 GB install and 714 MB resident (a second process).
* *The int8 Kokoro export was 10 times slower than fp32* on this CPU (RTF 4.3). The cause was not investigated (this is the onnxruntime CPU provider with 4 intra-op threads); the figure is reported as measured, and it is why fp32 is the Kokoro row that matters.
* *Intelligibility proxy.* The reading CER of Supertonic, Kokoro fp32 and VOICEVOX is 2-4% and their intervals overlap; VOICEVOX has the lowest point estimate. The text CER (8-13%) is dominated by spelling choices of the STT and does not order the engines. Supertonic's seeded and unseeded runs differ by 2.2 points of text CER (8.2 against 10.4) from generation noise alone, which is the size of the differences between engines: *the ordering among the three is not established*.

=== 5.3 Supertonic needs explicit language and seed

In sherpa-onnx 2.0.1 the Supertonic language comes from +GenerationConfig.extra["lang"]+ and defaults to +en+ (source read: +offline-tts-supertonic-impl.cc+); the seed comes from +extra["seed"]+ and defaults to -1, a fresh random seed (a replay-relevant default). Measured: with +lang+ unset the same Japanese text gave shorter audio (mean 3.65 s against 4.18 s) and the CER rose (14.7% against 8.2% text, 7.9% against 4.0% reading); unseeded runs were identical on 0 of 50 replies in a process and across processes. Seeded runs on the Python wheel and on the Rust crate were bit-identical (section 7).

=== 5.4 sherpa-onnx Kokoro does not speak Japanese

The shipped +kokoro-multi-lang-v1_0+ archive contains +lexicon-us-en.txt+, +lexicon-gb-en.txt+ and +lexicon-zh.txt+ plus +espeak-ng-data+, which sherpa-onnx 2.0.1 no longer uses. With +lang=ja+ the library logged "OOV character skipped" for kana, and the 50 replies came out as 0.7-2 s of unintelligible audio (CER 97.8%, 32 of 32 handling items misread). It is a library and data limitation, not a measurement artefact; a Japanese Kokoro through sherpa-onnx would need a Japanese lexicon or pre-phonemized input (the Rust and Python APIs accept +phoneme_codepoints+, not measured here).

=== 6.2 Which items

* *Both VOICEVOX and Supertonic:* "1日目" (ついたち-め expected) came back as 一日目 for both: the STT cannot tell the readings apart, so this item is a proxy blind spot, not evidence of a misreading by either engine.
* *Supertonic (6, +ja+ seeded):* 18.5度 came back as 百八十五度 (expected じゅうはってんごど; Kokoro fp32 gave the same text, so the decimal point is probably spoken without "点" or lost: an item to listen to), 1日目 (see above), +YouTube+ (ゆりゆうび), +Twitter+ and +X+ (トイレル、アイクス), +VTuber+ (BTビル), and the kanji word 海老 (回廊: a real error of the model or of the STT). Ordinary kanji words, dates, counters, prices, years and 二十歳 passed.
* *VOICEVOX (2):* 1日目 (see above) and "Thank you" (サンクユー, a rendering of the English phrase the proxy scores as a miss).
* *Kokoro fp32 (11):* the same 18.5度 and 1日目, and all eight English items (+YouTube+, +OpenAI+, +Twitter/X+, +VTuber+, +BGM+, +Discord+, +Thank you+, +AI+) plus one kanji item (今日は昨日より明日 read あす).
* ADR-0004 assumption A-SPEECH-002 ("a TTS backend's own Japanese frontend reads ordinary kanji correctly enough that the baseline projection need not rewrite ordinary text") is *supported for ordinary kanji by all three Japanese-capable engines* (kanji misreadings 0, 1, 1 of 14) and *not supported for English words and brands* (3, 1, 8 of 8): the lexicon of ADR-0004 stage 1 needs Latin entries, as D-5 already says ("unknown Latin words pass through ... counted"). The proxy is blind to homograph choices, so this is weaker evidence than a listening review.

=== 6.3 Non-speech text (raw engine behaviour)

* *URLs* were *spoken* by every engine and badly: Supertonic "STPSEXIB", VOICEVOX "happSEXUPELコムライブ" (6.1 s of audio for one sentence), Kokoro garbled; VOICEVOX needed 6.1 s and 7.7 s of audio for the two URL sentences against a 4.2 s mean for the replies. ADR-0004 D-5 (URLs are not spoken, dropped from +speech_text+) is *necessary*.
* *Emoji and kaomoji* were *silently dropped* by every engine tested (no error, no crash; the hypothesis for 🎉🎉🎉 was just "やった"), so a non-deleting projection would not damage audio but the deletion should still be done by the project (an emoji could carry a different tone elsewhere).
* *Symbols* (&, %, #, @): VOICEVOX read them as words ("えやんど", "パーセント", "アット"), Supertonic partly, Kokoro mostly dropped them.

=== 6.4 Determinism

Seeded Supertonic and VOICEVOX are bit-identical across 50 replies in a process and across two fresh processes (same machine, same thread count; the thread count was not varied for TTS, unlike EXP-0002's STT result, so replay should still pin it). Unseeded Supertonic and both Kokoro runs are not repeatable: for Kokoro fp32 two syntheses of the same sentence differ in length (64463 against 64224 samples) and in samples (maximum absolute difference 0.094 on a peak of 0.44). The cause was not isolated (random duration or excitation noise is the probable mechanism). For replay the project would record the produced +audio_ref+ and its content digest and not rely on re-synthesis, which is what the existing contract already does (the asset carries +audio_ref+); determinism only buys reproducible cache keys and regression tests.

== 9. Proposed integration shape (for S2b; not decided here)

. *Adapter unchanged.* The production +NormalizedHttpTtsAdapter+ stays the client. +AIVTUBER_TTS_ENDPOINT=http://127.0.0.1:<port>/...+ satisfies the +local+ profile's loopback-only rule and the adapter's "HTTPS or localhost HTTP" check; no TTS credential (the adapter's key is optional).
. *Sidecar binary* (name tentative, +speech-tts-sidecar+) in +crates/adapters+, behind an off-by-default feature that reuses the +sherpa-stt+ pins and the supervised-child pattern of +speech-stt-worker+. One loaded model, a bounded request queue (reject with a typed non-200 when full, which the adapter already maps), one synthesis at a time (concurrency not measured).
. *Request:* the adapter's body {+text+, +style+, +voice_model+, +viseme_mapping+}. +text+ is the ADR-0004 +speech_text+ (projection and output gate done upstream; the sidecar never sees a URL or an emoji). +voice_model+ selects the Supertonic voice index; +style+ is not used in the first slice (open: Q6).
. *Response:* the sidecar writes the audio as +<AUDIO_ROOT>/generated/<digest>.wav+ (16-bit PCM, 44.1 kHz; the default +ffplay+ audio command plays it) through a temp file and an atomic rename, and answers {+audio_ref+: +audio://generated/<digest>.wav+, +duration_ms+}. +digest+ is a content address of (speech text, language, seed, steps, speed, voice index, model pin): the same request reuses the file, replay and cache keys are stable, and the seed is derived from the text digest so no random seed is used. +viseme_ref+ is absent (Supertonic produces no visemes: Q7).
. *Bounds:* the generated-audio directory needs a size or age cap and garbage collection (not designed here); text length is bounded by the existing ADR-0004 and output-gate limits (the sidecar should also cap it; Supertonic chunks Japanese text at 120 characters).
. *Failure:* sidecar down, slow (deadline from the interaction budget), or queue full degrade through the existing typed fallback (non-verbal reaction); the deterministic performer never waits on it.
. *Model identity* in provenance: +backend_name+ = the sidecar, +model_alias+ = +supertonic-3-int8+, +model_version+ = the pinned archive digest, so +provenance.tts+ stays replayable.
. *License surface:* the operator-facing startup summary names the voice model and license (OpenRAIL-M, disclosure duty), and for a VOICEVOX backend the credit line to display.
. *CI:* fixtures and a mock TTS only; no model download in CI (matches #220's acceptance criteria); the sidecar's own test uses a stub engine, the real-model check stays an operator step like the STT model fetch.

== 10. Limitations

. *No naturalness evidence.* There was no listening test, MOS, prosody or accent evaluation, and no human listener. The round-trip CER shows that an STT can recover the words; it says nothing about voice quality, pitch accent or style. The engine ordering by CER is within noise (section 5.2).
. *STT errors count against the TTS.* The ReazonSpeech STT was tuned on and validated with read speech (EXP-0002: reported CER 10-19% on Common Voice there); its error floor on synthetic speech was not measured, so absolute CER is pessimistic and a different STT would change the numbers.
. *The reading metric has blind spots:* homograph choices that share a spelling cannot be told apart (1日目, 二十歳), and Open JTalk reads both sides, so an error that Open JTalk makes on both is invisible. The 0.25 threshold for a "misreading" is arbitrary.
. *Small, synthetic workload:* 50 replies and 38 handling items, written by one author, 12-43 characters, no long monologue, no conversational disfluencies, one voice per engine (Supertonic sid 0 only; the other nine voices and the other VOICEVOX characters were not measured).
. *Seed variance:* Supertonic's seeded and unseeded runs differed by 2.2 points of text CER; only one seed was run.
. *One machine and one run per configuration*, Windows only, CPU only, no GPU numbers, no concurrency (a synthesis while STT, the renderer or the LLM run was not measured; the S1 note on contention applies).
. *Cause of several effects not isolated:* the 10 times slowdown of the int8 Kokoro export, the Kokoro non-determinism, the Supertonic misreading of 海老 and the decimal item.
. *Licenses were read from cards, READMEs and shipped files, not from legal advice.* The Supertonic model's training data, the Kokoro G2P chain licenses (pyopenjtalk, unidic-lite, mojimoji), Style-Bert-VITS2's default terms, the Irodori codec's upstream license and the per-character full terms pages were not read.
. *Supertonic export provenance:* the sherpa-onnx int8 archive is a third-party conversion; it was not compared with the HF files.
. *Not run:* Irodori-TTS, MeloTTS-JP, Qwen3-TTS, Chatterbox, GPT-SoVITS, AivisSpeech Engine, Style-Bert-VITS2, Piper. They were assessed from licenses and cards only.

== 11. Protocol deviations

* *No GPU run.* The brief made GPU optional; nothing was run on the GPU.
* *Candidates read but not run:* section 8, with reasons (license, or a GPU-class torch stack with no CPU figures).
* *Reply length:* the planned range was 5-60 characters; the 50 replies span 12-43 characters (section 4.1).
* *Kokoro Japanese G2P on Windows:* +misaki[ja]+ could not be installed as published (+pyopenjtalk+ has no Windows wheel for Python 3.13 and +mojimoji+ needs a C compiler, which the machine lacked). The bench used +pyopenjtalk-plus+ (a maintained fork with a wheel) and a pure-Python stand-in for the two +mojimoji+ calls misaki 0.7.4 makes (+examples/benchmarks/tts/shims/mojimoji.py+). This may differ from the upstream stack in edge cases and is a reason the Kokoro rows are weaker evidence.
* *Harness bug fixed and rerun:* the first kokoro-onnx runs failed on all 50 replies (a tuple unpacking of the G2P result); they were fixed and rerun, and only the rerun is reported. The Supertonic and VOICEVOX runs were not affected.
* *Not every run was repeated:* the default-language Supertonic and the sherpa Kokoro rows have one run (no fresh-process comparison).
* *Probe hashing detail:* the Rust and Python audio were compared as float32 (bit-identical). A first comparison on 16-bit PCM hashes differed in one sample LSB because of different rounding at exact halves; the float comparison is the one reported.
* *VOICEVOX models* were not pinned separately from the engine archive; the archive hash pins the whole bundle.

== 12. Open questions

. *Q1 (OpenRAIL-M).* Is the project prepared to ship or recommend a model under OpenRAIL-M, including the enforceable use restrictions passed downstream and the duty to disclose that published audio is machine-generated (relevant to a stream; where would the disclosure live: overlay, stream description, or the startup notice)? If not, the fallbacks are VOICEVOX with the credit, or a benchmark of Irodori-TTS (MIT).
. *Q2 (default engine).* Is a Supertonic default with VOICEVOX as an optional operator-chosen backend the right split, or should VOICEVOX be the default because its measured reading was best and it has the most Japanese voices?
. *Q3 (scope of S2b).* Sidecar as a Rust binary over the +sherpa-onnx+ crate (this record's recommendation), or the Python wheel as a stopgap sidecar for the first slice? (The second needs no Rust build but ships a Python dependency; both speak the same contract.)
. *Q4 (VOICEVOX character).* Which character, if VOICEVOX is offered, and may the persona use a third-party mascot voice? Several characters restrict commercial use.
. *Q5 (naturalness check).* A human listening comparison (a handful of replies, Supertonic voices against VOICEVOX) is needed before any engine is called good; who listens and with what rubric? Irodori-TTS and other 0.5-2 B models enter only if this check fails.
. *Q6 (+style+).* The contract carries an optional +style+; Supertonic has none and VOICEVOX has styles per character. Do we map +style+ to a voice/speed preset, or ignore it in the first slice?
. *Q7 (lip sync).* The renderer path needs visemes or an amplitude envelope; neither engine returns +viseme_ref+. Is amplitude-driven mouth movement from the audio acceptable for the local avatar (#219)?
. *Q8 (hardware).* Does the target machine have spare CPU for a 4-thread synthesis next to STT and the avatar renderer? Contention was not measured.
