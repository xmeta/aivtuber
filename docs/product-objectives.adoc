= Product Objectives and Guardrails
:toc:
:toclevels: 2

This document is the canonical product-objective and proxy-governance reference for aivtuber.
It complements link:metrics.adoc[Metrics and Evaluation]: metrics describe observations, while this document describes what those observations are intended to protect or improve.

== 1. Primary product objective

Produce contextually appropriate, natural, timely, and operationally safe AI VTuber reactions to stream events while avoiding unnecessary dependence on slow or expensive external generation.

The intended beneficiaries are viewers receiving the performance and the operator responsible for running, diagnosing, and stopping the system.
Material third-party effects include privacy, platform/community safety, provider usage, and resource/network externalities.

The objective is deliberately multi-dimensional.
No single latency, cache, cost, or model-usage metric is a substitute for the product objective.

== 2. Secondary objectives

Within the hard guardrails below, prefer changes that:

* reduce avoidable interaction latency;
* reduce unnecessary LLM/TTS/provider calls and stream-hour cost;
* increase safe reuse of validated Performance Assets;
* preserve useful reaction variety and avoid inappropriate repetition;
* improve long-running reliability and graceful degradation;
* improve observability, deterministic replay, and reproducibility;
* keep operator intervention fast and predictable.

Secondary objectives may trade off against one another.
A change is not an unqualified improvement merely because one secondary objective improves.

== 3. Hard guardrails

The following are constraints, not performance scores to trade away:

* security and authorization boundaries remain intact;
* serialized/model-provided claims never mint runtime authority;
* operator stop/mute remains independently effective under content/provider failure or saturation;
* stale or cancelled actions do not dispatch after their cancellation fence;
* privacy and durable-memory write policy remain enforced;
* replay-relevant decisions remain deterministic where the contract requires it;
* long-lived queues, histories, caches, observations, and retained context remain explicitly bounded;
* required degraded paths do not freeze the deterministic performer.

A candidate that violates a hard guardrail is not made acceptable by better latency, cost, reuse, throughput, or subjective quality.

== 4. Proxy-metric governance

Important optimization proxies must be interpreted with counter-metrics or guardrails.

[cols="2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
|Optimization proxy
|What it can miss
|Required counter-metric / guardrail

|Cache / semantic reuse hit rate
|Contextually wrong or stale reuse
|Wrong-reuse labels; reaction appropriateness; semantic near-miss review

|LLM / TTS avoidance
|Missing useful responses or overusing canned behavior
|Reaction-quality review; intentional-silence vs unintended-silence outcomes; fallback reason

|Event-to-first-audio / visible latency
|Awkward interruption, premature response, or unnatural timing
|Timing/interruption quality review; cancellation and operator-override behavior

|Template utilization
|Repetition or contextually poor template selection
|Repetition measures; reaction appropriateness; semantic/template error labels

|Generation latency
|Higher failure rate, degraded output, or stale completion
|Generation failure/degradation rate; stale-dispatch invariant; cancellation outcome

|Throughput
|Silent overload, excessive drop, or control-path starvation
|Drop/backpressure counters; priority outcome; emergency-control responsiveness

|Lower retained-state footprint
|Excess eviction, regeneration, cache misses, or quality loss
|Cache/regeneration metrics; latency/cost changes; configured-bound invariants

|Estimated cost
|Quality loss or unmeasured provider work
|Quality guardrails; call counts; explicit "unmeasured" rather than zero when inputs are absent
|===

When a new optimization metric becomes decision-relevant, its owning issue/PR should name what important product quality the metric does not measure and identify a corresponding counter-metric or hard guardrail before it is treated as a target.

== 5. Qualities not fully captured by runtime counters

Current deterministic telemetry is necessary but not sufficient for product quality.
Important qualities that require offline or human-reviewed evidence include, as applicable:

* contextual reaction appropriateness;
* naturalness and timing appropriateness;
* repetition and behavior variety;
* personality consistency;
* interruption/preemption quality;
* semantic near-miss and wrong-reuse severity;
* whether silence, deferral, or non-verbal acknowledgement was preferable to a spoken response.

link:reaction-quality-evaluation.adoc[Reaction-Quality Evaluation Dataset] defines the versioned scenario/holdout boundary owned by #59.
Until reviewed evidence exists for a dimension, do not claim that a timing/cost/reuse improvement proves that dimension improved.

== 6. Relationship to measurement

link:metrics.adoc[Metrics and Evaluation] defines the production observations and replay comparison boundary.
link:performance-goals.adoc[Performance Goals and Baselines] distinguishes regression budgets from product targets.

Issue #58 supplies quantitative comparison infrastructure.
A comparable base-vs-head measurement can show that a proxy changed; it does not by itself prove that the primary product objective improved.

Numeric product targets and operational SLOs must be derived from representative evidence and measured baselines.
Do not invent absolute thresholds in this document; #70 and #71 own representative workloads and operational SLO calibration.
link:operational-slo.adoc[Operational SLOs and Error Budgets] owns the operational half: which events form a denominator, which failures belong to the runtime rather than to a provider, and why a security or bounded-state invariant never receives an error budget.

== 7. Proxy divergence and review

Treat the following as evidence that a proxy may be diverging from the product objective:

* a proxy improves while its counter-metric or quality review regresses;
* operators compensate manually more often despite "better" benchmark numbers;
* a policy learns to satisfy a benchmark fixture while degrading held-out or live behavior;
* an optimization shifts cost/latency to an unmeasured stage;
* metric definitions or workload composition change enough that historical comparisons are no longer like-for-like;
* repeated qualitative incidents are not visible in the metrics used to justify the change.

When this happens:

. do not describe the change as an unqualified improvement;
. identify whether the metric, workload, guardrail, or intervention is wrong/incomplete;
. create or link the focused measurement/quality issue;
. use the expected-vs-actual / decision workflow from #60;
. revise or retire the proxy if it no longer tracks the intended product quality.

Do not silently loosen a guardrail to preserve an optimization result.

== 8. Revisit conditions

Review this objective model when:

* #59 quality evaluation exposes a missing product-quality dimension;
* #70 representative workloads materially change the measured behavior;
* #71 operational SLO evidence shows persistent failure not represented here;
* a significant #62 after-action review identifies a missing guardrail or misleading proxy;
* a new Stage 3-5 capability materially changes who is affected or what "useful reaction" means;
* a security/privacy/platform constraint changes.

Changes to the primary objective or hard guardrails require an issue and explicit review rather than being hidden inside a local optimization PR.
