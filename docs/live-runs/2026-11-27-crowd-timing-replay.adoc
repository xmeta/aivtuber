= Deterministic crowd-timing replay evidence, 2026-11-27
:toc:

Offline, deterministic replay evidence for the https://github.com/xmeta/aivtuber/issues/79[#79] crowd stages and the https://github.com/xmeta/aivtuber/issues/489[#489, v0.3 Watchable Hour] evidence gap, produced without a live run.
Raw session text and actor identifiers are not reproduced here; only counts and logical intervals.

== What this is, and what it is not

* It is a replay of the production burst/audience/crowd lane and its composition path (`process_content_bytes`, `service_chat_burst`, deferred inbox, session log #463) over a checked-in scenario, summarized by the same offline reader the live runs use (`aivtuberctl log summary`, #494 stages).
* It is **not** a live run and **not** representative-hour evidence. It holds no generation worker, no audio device and no wall clock: every instant is the fixture's own logical milliseconds, and a phrase interval is a recorded commit, not acoustic or visible onset.
* It therefore cannot by itself move link:../current-constraint.adoc[the current measured constraint]. It bounds the shipped pack-answered path and gives #489 a deterministic before/after instrument that runs in under a second.

== Scenario

`examples/crowd-routing/timing-scenario.json` (`crowd-routing-timing-v1`) keeps the shape of `examples/crowd-routing/falsifier.json`: `window_ms=1500`, `burst_enter_count=2`, `top_k=8`, `max_per_actor=4`, `max_candidates=64`, `reply_ttl_ms=20000`, crowd window `60000`, crowd cooldown `20000`.

Seven waves are spaced 30 s apart, beyond the crowd cooldown, so each routes exactly once: five celebration waves (steps 5, 20, 60, 15 and 25 ms), one laughter wave and one repeated-question wave. A final pair overlaps on purpose (laughter arrives 1.5 s after a celebration); both are still dispatched at their routing instant.

== Reproduction

[source,sh]
----
cargo test -p aivtuber-app --locked --lib \
  deterministic_timing_scenario_reports_crowd_stage_distributions -- --nocapture
----

The test replays the scenario twice (asserting a byte-identical summary), prints the offline summary, and writes the per-route stages to `target/crowd-routing-timing/crowd-routing-timing-v1.json` (not committed).

== What it shows

* **`route_observation_to_dispatch` is 0 for every route** in this scenario, including the representative reply and the wave that overlapped a speaking performer. That matches the #79 S5 property: a carrier the pack answers is dispatched at the routing instant, and with no generation worker held there is no queue to wait behind. The 3,175 ms Track A wait is therefore a queue effect, not crowd work, and this replay reproduces its *absence* deterministically.
* **`route_observation_to_first_phrase` is 83–127 ms** once the carrier is dispatched from the pack; the variation comes from scheduling offset, not from selection.
* **`input_to_route_observation` is dominated by the batch window** (~1.5 s) for isolated waves. The p95/max of 25,080 ms is the final overlapping wave, and it is a harness artifact, not a held or suppressed route: the replay settles every phase for a fixed horizon (100 steps of 250 ms) before feeding the next, so the last wave's arrivals are clamped to a logical clock already about 23.5 s ahead. It routes on the final settle step, is dispatched at that same instant (`route_observation_to_dispatch` 0), and the replay ends before its phrase is committed. Nothing expired and the crowd cooldown did not apply (`crowd_unvoiced=0`, no recorded end reason), so its missing phrase interval is a missing endpoint rather than the unvoiced-route case.

== Limitations

* Logical time only; no acoustic playback, no visible acknowledgement, no provider or TTS wall clock.
* One scenario shape (pack-answered celebrations and laughter plus one question) on one lane configuration; it does not cover other crowd classes, other `top_k`/window settings, or a held generation worker (the dedicated #79 S5 unit tests cover that case and also observe 0 ms).
* The final wave's 25 s input→route interval reflects the replay's per-phase settle horizon and the resulting clamp of its arrival times, not a deferred TTL, a crowd cooldown or a measured live wait.

Revisit when a generation-holding scenario is needed to reproduce the Track A queue wait offline, or when the lane defaults change (the scenario must then be re-baselined rather than pooled).
